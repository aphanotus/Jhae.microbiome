This is the fastest option for non-GIS users because it has a built-in function to draw a perfect **1-square-kilometer box** around any point and instantly calculate the land cover percentages. [[1](https://nrca.media.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3424/2022/05/10_TPL_ModelMyWatershedExercise_FINAL_2018.pdf)]

- **How to use it:**

  1. Open the [Model My Watershed Application](https://modelmywatershed.org/) in your browser.
  2. Use the map search bar to navigate to your first specific location (e.g., an address or coordinates in Miami or Lehigh Acres).
  3. In the upper-right control panel under "Select Area," click **Draw Area**.
  4. Choose the **Square Km** option from the dropdown menu.
  5. Click directly on your exact location on the map. The tool will automatically generate a 1-square-km bounding box centered on that point.
  6. A data analysis window will pop up on the left side of the screen. Click on the **Land** tab.
  7. Look at the chart and table. It will give you the exact percentage breakdown of **Developed, High Intensity** (80%–100% impervious cover), **Developed, Medium Intensity** (50%–79%), and **Developed, Low Intensity** (20%–49%). You can sum or average these values to get a robust estimate of localized urban density. [[1](https://wikiwatershed.org/model-my-watershed-release-1-27/), [2](https://www.baybackpack.com/files/4.5.6-Model-My-Watershed®-Overview-FINAL.pdf), [3](https://wikiwatershed.org/knowledge-base/free-draw-a-polygon-area-around-watershed/), [4](https://hrnerr.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Model-Your-Watershed.pdf), [5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hSkGAeVHA4c)]

  

MRLC NLCD Viewer

The Multi-Resolution Land Characteristics (MRLC) Consortium provides an official interactive map viewer that hosts the ultimate U.S. database for imperviousness. [[1](https://www.mrlc.gov/), [2](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=2fc5690dfe6949f88c1a2b9bcd584e46)]

- **How to use it:**

  1. Go to the [MRLC Viewer](https://www.mrlc.gov/viewer/).
  2. In the layer controls on the right, turn on the **Fractional Impervious Surface** layer. This maps the specific percentage of paved or built surfaces (0% to 100%) for every 30-meter pixel on the landscape.
  3. Navigate to your South Florida sites. The map will color-code the ground based on the intensity of the impermeable surface.
  4. You can use the viewer's built-in tool to draw a rectangle or custom polygon over your site. The app allows you to view localized zonal statistics directly in your browser window. [[1](https://www.mrlc.gov/viewer/), [2](https://www.mrlc.gov/data/type/urban-imperviousness), [3](https://www.mrlc.gov/data/type/fractional-impervious-surface), [4](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=2fc5690dfe6949f88c1a2b9bcd584e46), [5](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=6df535f263dd44f489365eed49461a38)]

  

  i-Tree Canopy (For Statistical Sampling)

  If you want to manually verify the land cover types down to individual driveways, trees, and sidewalks within a 1-square-mile boundary, you can use a tool developed by the US Forest Service.

  - **How to use it:**

    1. Open [i-Tree Canopy](https://canopy.itreetools.org/).
    2. Use their browser boundary tool to outline a square mile around your site.
    3. The tool will randomly distribute photo-interpretation pins across that area.
    4. You look at the Google Maps satellite view for each pin and classify it (e.g., "Impervious Road", "Impervious Roof", "Grass", or "Tree").
    5. After classifying 50–100 random points, the tool automatically calculates the exact proportion of impermeable surface per unit area along with a statistical margin of error.
